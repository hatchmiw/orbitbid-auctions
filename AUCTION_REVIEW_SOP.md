# OrbitBid auction opportunity SOP v3

**Goal:** identify promising auction lots, investigate them sufficiently to support the user's own bidding decisions, and deliver a concise actionable shortlist. **Do not require individual-original-photo inspection or market research for every catalog lot.** The auction-specific `REVIEW.md` defines the current scope. Preserve historical research and inspection evidence; do not reinterpret contact-sheet screening as full-original inspection.

## 1. Acquire and screen the whole catalog
Read this SOP, `AGENTS.md`, the auction's `REVIEW.md`, the master `catalog-review.csv` and existing batch findings. Import or refresh all lots, descriptions, links, source photo counts, initial photographs and current bids using the existing workflows. Reuse prior contact-sheet observations for rapid triage, not as full inspections. Screen every catalog lot once for recognizable brands/models, likely demand, personal-use value, shipping/pickup practicality, damage, missing parts, buyer fees and obvious exclusions. Use `High`, `Medium` and `Low` as **screening priorities**, not valuations or final recommendations. Revisit medium/low lots when evidence suggests overlooked value.

## 2. Build an opportunity queue
Select promising lots based on identifiable value, likely buyer demand, condition, fees, handling and the user's stated interests. Do not force quotas or manufacture opportunities. Separate `personal use` from `resale` and allow both. Work toward a first deliverable of roughly 10 genuinely investigated candidates, then expand to the remaining promising lots. For each selected lot, record why it merits investigation and the decisive unanswered questions.

## 3. Inspect shortlisted lots in depth
For a shortlisted lot, reuse source-verified original photos from existing GitHub Actions artifacts. Examine **every original JPG for that lot individually**, verify the source lot ID and count, identify brands/models/quantities/components, visible condition and defects, and explicitly distinguish observed condition from tested operation. Contact sheets are acceptable for catalog screening but never certify a detailed original-photo inspection. For other lots, retain their truthful screened-only state. Mark no-photo lots as catalog-only and escalate only if catalog information justifies research. Never commit photographs, ZIPs or contact sheets to Git.

## 4. Research actual market value
For shortlisted lots, seek dated, comparable **completed sales** matched by exact model, condition, quantity and accessories; record sale price, date, venue and URL. Asking listings and new retail prices are context only, never completed-sale evidence. Compare personal-use replacement cost separately from likely resale proceeds. For resale, account for fees, buyer premium, tax, pickup, repair, shipping, selling fees, time and target margin. Record a conditional gross resale range and maximum hammer bid only when evidence and lot condition support them; otherwise clearly mark `evidence insufficient` and leave active values blank. Existing unverified historical estimates remain historical only.

## 5. Deliver an actionable shortlist
Maintain the single master `auctions/<id>/catalog-review.csv` as the editable per-lot source. Publish one concise auction-specific opportunity view with lot URL and photo URL, item identification, intended use (personal/resale/both), inspection findings, relevant sold comparables and dates, estimated resale/replacement value where supported, conditional maximum hammer bid where supported, critical risks, current bid timestamp and user decision status. The shortlist is a research aid, not an automatic instruction to bid. Do not inflate the shortlist with insufficiently researched lots; separate `research in progress` from `ready for user review`.

## 6. Save, recover and refresh
After each **completed candidate inspection or researched valuation**, write its findings into the master and verify the GitHub commit before starting another substantial task. For rapid screening, save small batches frequently; do not waste the session making a commit per discarded lot. Validate 487 unique records for auction 1879, all required columns, unchanged historical evidence and source photo counts. Update one auction-specific `REVIEW.md` with the actual next research candidate and blockers. Regenerate derived views from the master where supported; do not create competing checkpoint reports. Commits are checkpoints, not stopping conditions. On interruption, resume from committed master evidence and skip completed work.

## 7. Finish according to decision usefulness
The first milestone is a **usable, evidence-backed first shortlist**, not a nominal count of fully photographed lots. Expand the research queue as time permits and refresh current bids close to closing. Clearly report screened counts, detailed inspections, researched candidates, ready-for-review candidates and remaining uncertainties **as separate metrics**. Do not claim an auction is fully researched because the catalog was screened or images were downloaded.

## Change control
Keep this universal SOP concise. Auction-specific lot IDs, photo artifact maps, fee assumptions, research queues and exact checkpoints belong in `auctions/<id>/REVIEW.md`. Do not redesign working ingestion pipelines or add redundant reports during routine review.
